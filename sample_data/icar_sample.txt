ICAR Advisory: Wheat Crop Management (Illustrative Sample)

This is an illustrative advisory written for the starter project. It is not a
copy of any real ICAR bulletin; replace it with genuine advisory pages when you
go live.

Yellowing of wheat leaves. Uniform yellowing of the lower, older leaves during
the tillering and jointing stages most commonly indicates nitrogen deficiency.
Nitrogen is mobile in the plant, so the crop pulls it from older leaves to feed
new growth, which is why the oldest leaves yellow first. Where nitrogen
deficiency is confirmed, a top dressing of urea at about 50 kg per acre, applied
to moist soil and followed by light irrigation, usually restores green colour
within seven to ten days. Avoid applying urea to a dry field, as losses through
volatilisation are high.

Distinguishing look-alike problems. Yellowing from water stress tends to appear
first at the leaf tips and margins and worsens during the hottest part of the
day; correcting irrigation scheduling is the remedy. Interveinal yellowing while
the veins stay green (chlorosis) points instead to a micronutrient issue such as
zinc or iron, and a soil test to check pH is advised before applying any
micronutrient, because high soil pH can lock these nutrients away.

Leaf rust. Small, round to oval, orange-brown pustules scattered on the upper
leaf surface are the classic sign of leaf rust. Rust develops fastest when
relative humidity stays above seventy percent and temperatures sit in the range
of fifteen to twenty-five degrees Celsius, conditions that often follow a spell
of cloudy, damp weather. Scout fields twice a week during such periods. If
pustules are spreading, a recommended fungicide should be applied promptly, as
rust can move quickly through a susceptible variety and cause significant yield
loss.

Harvest and storage timing. Harvest wheat when the grain is hard and moisture
has fallen to a safe level, and always choose dry conditions for both harvesting
and threshing. Grain stored while damp is prone to mould and to storage pests,
so if rain is forecast it is safer to complete harvesting beforehand or to
ensure covered, well-ventilated storage. When market prices are rising but wet
weather is expected, farmers without proper moisture-protected storage should
weigh the risk of storage damage against the possible gain from holding the
crop.

General guidance. Recommendations should be adjusted to local soil test results,
the specific variety grown, and the current season. When symptoms persist after
treatment, farmers are advised to contact their local agricultural officer for
soil and leaf testing before applying further inputs.
